There's a moment in every era where a stat line stops being impressive and starts being inevitable. For Nikola Jokic, that moment arrived somewhere around his 30th triple-double of the 2025-26 season.

{{< stat-callout player="Nikola Jokic" stat="26.4 PPG" context="2025-26 Season Average" >}}

## The Numbers Don't Lie

Jokic isn't just accumulating triple-doubles — he's doing it with an efficiency that makes the historical comparisons feel unfair. His usage rate sits at 29.8% while maintaining a true shooting percentage above 65%. For context, the last center to post those splits while averaging double-digit assists was... nobody. It's never happened.

{{< stat-callout player="Nikola Jokic" stat="10.8 APG" context="Highest by a center since Wilt" >}}

## Why This Season Is Different

Previous Jokic triple-double seasons felt like a byproduct of his all-around game. This season feels intentional. The Nuggets have restructured their offense to run nearly everything through Jokic at the elbow, and the results have been devastating for opposing defenses.

The key change? Jokic's assist-to-turnover ratio has climbed to 3.8:1, up from 3.2:1 last season. He's not just passing more — he's passing *better*. His turnover rate in the half-court has dropped to 8.2%, the lowest mark of his career.

## Historical Context

Oscar Robertson's 1961-62 season — 41 triple-doubles — has long been considered untouchable. Russell Westbrook came closest with 42 in 2016-17, but that came with a usage rate north of 41% and significant efficiency questions.

{{< stat-callout player="Nikola Jokic" stat="34" context="Triple-doubles through 72 games" >}}

Jokic is on pace for 38 with ten games remaining. He'd need to rattle off four in his final stretch to match Robertson — unlikely but not impossible given he's had stretches of five in a row this season.

> This might be the most complete offensive season by a center in NBA history. We're not watching a stat-padder. We're watching a player whose game naturally produces triple-doubles because he's genuinely the best passer, scorer, and rebounder on his team every single night.

## The Bottom Line

Whether Jokic catches Robertson is almost beside the point. What he's doing this season — the efficiency, the playmaking, the sheer volume of production — cements him as the most unique offensive player in modern NBA history. There is no template for what Jokic is. He's a 7-foot point guard with post moves and a feathery three-point touch, and the numbers say he's having the best season of his career.

The triple-double record might fall. But even if it doesn't, this is a season worth remembering.
